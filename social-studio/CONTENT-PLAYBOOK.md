# Seacoast DJ content playbook

## Fast event capture routine

For each event, aim for:

1. One wide setup/room shot before guests arrive.
2. Two or three peak-energy dance floor shots.
3. One detail shot of the booth, lighting, or gear.
4. One shot that shows the crowd's energy without focusing on any one identifiable face.
5. One slow vertical video, 8–15 seconds, steady movement, no guest names or faces in focus.

## Before uploading

- Remove name signage, seating charts, guest books, and anything with a full name visible.
- Avoid house numbers, license plates, and venue signage that reveals the exact address unless approved.
- Confirm you have permission to use the images commercially (check the couple's/host's contract terms).
- Do not identify the exact venue unless the client explicitly approved it.
- Keep the original media backed up outside this application.

## Useful project details

- General town or service area
- Vertical (Wedding, Corporate, Private, Rental)
- Equipment or setup notes
- Special features (uplighting, photo booth, live mixing)
- Crowd size or energy level
- One reason the night worked (what made this event's dance floor fill up)

## Brand voice starter

Seacoast DJ should sound capable, high-energy, and proud of 20+ years of live mixing — never generic or templated. Short, benefit-forward sentences, second person ("you"), "&" instead of "and." Lead with proof (200+ weddings, 500+ events, licensed and insured) over vague superlatives. One primary call to action per post: Check Availability at SeacoastDJ.com.

## Weekly content session

Prioritize three reusable assets per week:

- A dance-floor/energy showcase (Instagram/Facebook)
- A behind-the-booth or gear detail post (Educational)
- A short vertical highlight Reel

Rotate across the four verticals so no single vertical (usually Wedding) dominates the feed — Corporate, Private, and Rental content all build credibility with different audiences.
